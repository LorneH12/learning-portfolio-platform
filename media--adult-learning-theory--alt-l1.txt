Before building it, ask what people must do and what prevents them from doing it.

Andragogy, associated with Malcolm Knowles, offers assumptions concerning adults' need to know why, self-direction, experience, readiness, problem orientation, and motivation. These are useful questions for design, not guarantees about each adult. An experienced employee may be a novice with this tool; someone attending a mandatory session may need substantial guidance. Adult learning has multiple theoretical perspectives, and andragogy is not its single universal explanation. See the Penn State readings in Sources.

Worked example: replace “They are adults, so let them explore” with “Can they already locate the owner list and submit a practice handoff?” Observe the task, ask about obstacles, and offer a guided example when needed.
