Allow 4 minutes. “Understand triage” communicates a broad intention but does not specify the evidence to collect. For this sample, make three elements explicit: the observable behavior, the conditions under which it happens, and the acceptable standard. Carnegie Mellon’s learning-objective guidance supports writing objectives that point to a performance that can be assessed.

Worked example: “Given the current rule card and four fictional support tickets, assign a priority to each ticket with at least three correct.” Behavior: assign priorities. Condition: four cases and the rule card. Standard: three of four correct. The numbers are illustrative design choices, not a validated safety threshold. A real organization must set standards appropriate to its risks.

Avoid piling incompatible performances into one objective. “List definitions, build an escalation workflow, and motivate colleagues” needs separate evidence. Also avoid false precision: a strict time limit is appropriate only when speed is genuinely part of the job and the measurement is fair.

Drafting pause, 60 seconds: rewrite “Know the handoff process.” Sample response: “Given a fictional handoff and the required-field checklist, identify all missing required elements.” Add a criterion that reflects the use case. Your draft is a self-check, not an automatically verified performance.
