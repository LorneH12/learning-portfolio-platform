Worked example: all Northstar figures are synthetic, in USD thousands, for the same quarter. Revenue is 600. Operating expenses excluding depreciation and amortization are 420. Depreciation is 30; amortization is 10. There is no other operating or non-operating income or expense. Interest expense is 20, there is no interest income, and income-tax expense is 36.

Top down: 600 − 420 − 30 − 10 = 140 EBIT. Then 140 − 20 − 36 = 84 net income.

Bottom up: 84 net income + 20 interest + 36 income tax = 140 EBIT. Add 30 depreciation + 10 amortization = 180 EBITDA. The bridge difference is 96, not a cash deposit. Check: 180 − 30 − 10 − 20 − 36 = 84.

EBITDA = EBIT + depreciation + amortization. In this simplified case, EBITDA = net income + interest expense + income-tax expense + depreciation + amortization. All add-backs were already deducted in net income. If a statement contains interest income, use the disclosed interest treatment consistently; removing net interest means adding expense and subtracting income. Other non-operating items can make EBIT different from operating income. Do not assume those terms always match.
